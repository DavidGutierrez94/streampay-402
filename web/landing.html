Pay only for the time you actually use.

Per-second onchain billing on Monad, built as an add-on to x402.

Compute, APIs, GPUs, streams, rentals: if it’s metered by time, the payer locks a deposit in escrow, the meter runs every second, and whatever wasn’t used is refunded the moment it stops.

Anything you meter by time.

  • AI agents

    per second of service

    An agent opens a stream, works, and closes it. It never pays for a minute it didn’t use. Live on Monad testnet.

  • Compute

    per second of runtime

    Jobs that finish early stop paying early. No hourly rounding.

  • APIs

    per second connected

    Long-running and streaming endpoints, priced by how long they’re held open.

  • GPUs

    per second of GPU time

    Rent an accelerator for the length of the job, not a reserved block.

  • Streams

    per second watched

    Video, audio or data feeds that charge while someone is actually tuned in.

  • Rentals

    per second borrowed

    Anything lent out by the clock, settled the moment it’s returned.

One escrow, one meter, one close.

  1. t = 0

    Open escrow

    A client, whether an agent, an app or a person, calls your endpoint and gets a 402 with your per-second terms. It locks a deposit in the StreamEscrow contract on Monad.

  2. t = 1, 2, 3 …

    Stream per second

    Your service does the work while the meter runs. What’s owed is always min(deposit, rate × elapsed), read from block time, never from a counter you have to trust.

  3. t = end

    Claim and close

    You claim what accrued. The client closes the stream, and the unused part of its deposit is refunded in the same transaction.

Standard x402, plus a clock.

Clients that already speak x402, starting with AI agents, see a familiar 402 Payment Required. The scheme says how to pay: open a stream, not a one-off transfer.

Providers sign up once, register a price per second in the ServiceRegistry, and authenticate with pk_/sk_ keys.
